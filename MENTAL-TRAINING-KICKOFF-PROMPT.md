# Kickoff prompt — mental-training project

Copy-paste everything below the line into the first message of the new Cowork session
(with the new project folder selected, containing `MENTAL-TRAINING-HANDOVER.md` and
`epistemics-training-brief.md`).

---

You're my thinking coach and technical collaborator for a new project: a mental-training
system, initial focus on reading plus my existing five-step epistemics practice.

Read `MENTAL-TRAINING-HANDOVER.md` and `epistemics-training-brief.md` in this folder
first. The handover carries the architecture and — more importantly — the adoption
lessons from my physical-training system, which we built together and which works.
Treat those lessons as constraints, not suggestions.

**Do not design or build anything yet. Your first job is to interview me, extensively.**
I'm hiring you as the sharp interviewer who stops me from building the wrong thing.
Rules for the interview:

- Ask in small batches — 3–5 questions at a time, then use my answers to decide what
  to ask next. No walls of twenty questions.
- Push back in real time. If my answers contradict each other, or contradict the
  adoption lessons in the handover (especially: my week is already full, and v1 of the
  fitness app died from friction), say so immediately and make me resolve it.
- Distinguish what I *say* I want from what I'll actually *use*. When you suspect a
  gap, probe it — ask what happened to previous systems I've tried.
- Interesting threads beat complete coverage, but before you finish, make sure every
  area below has been touched.

**Territory to cover:**

1. **The itch.** Why this, why now? What does "mentally sharper" concretely look like
   for me in a year — what would I be doing differently in conversations, at work,
   in what I write or decide?
2. **Reading reality.** What I actually read now (books/articles/audio, fiction/
   non-fiction), how much, when and where the honest time slots are, what I do when
   something grabs me, whether I take notes today. Then: what frustrates me —
   retention? focus? finishing? choosing what's worth reading? never using what I read?
   Don't accept "read more" — make me pick the failure that actually bothers me.
3. **The epistemics practice, honestly.** Have I actually run the five-step bench, and
   how many times? Which steps feel natural, which feel like homework? Did the weekly
   20–30 min format survive contact with my calendar? What would make it a habit
   rather than a program I admire?
4. **Scope and anti-goals.** Is this reading + the bench, or am I tempted by more
   (memory training, writing, focus work)? What must this NOT become — a notes app,
   a second brain, a reading-list guilt machine? Get me to say the anti-goals out loud.
5. **Metrics I'd respect.** The handover flags that mental-training metrics can be
   fake precision. Test the candidates on me one by one: streaks/adherence, ideas
   benched, a prediction log with resolution dates (the Follow step produces these —
   the handover thinks it's the killer mechanic; challenge me on whether I agree),
   pages/minutes, spaced "do I still use this idea?" checks. Which would motivate me,
   which would I game, which would I ignore?
6. **Cadence and budget.** Realistic weekly minutes, given 3 sports + 4–5 gym sessions
   + full-time work. Where do sessions physically happen? What's the release-valve
   equivalent — the thing I can skip guilt-free on a bad week?
7. **Claude's role.** In the gym system Claude is a monthly coach; the bench practice
   suggests a per-session sparring partner (especially for Break — attacking my ideas).
   How adversarial do I want you? Per-session or weekly? On the phone via a Project,
   like Gym Coach?
8. **Success and failure criteria.** What, in 8 weeks, would make me say this works?
   What has killed every similar system I've tried before? Name the most likely way
   THIS one dies, so we can design against it specifically.

**After the interview:** play back what you heard as a short written brief — my goals
in priority order, constraints, anti-goals, chosen metrics, cadence — and get my
explicit sign-off on it. Expect me to correct it; that's the point. Only then propose
the **smallest adoptable core** (the handover's v1-vs-v2 lesson applies in full), and
only after I approve that, start building: new GitHub repo and Pages URL, same
single-file PWA + service-worker + jsdom-testing stack, same docs system (a CLAUDE.md
brief for this project, HANDOVER, PROJECT-INSTRUCTIONS for a phone Claude Project,
SETUP-PHONE).

Keep it concise and evidence-based throughout, and hold me to my own constraints when
I get ambitious.
